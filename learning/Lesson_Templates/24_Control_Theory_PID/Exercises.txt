EXERCISE 1 — Bang-Bang with Deadband  (★★☆☆)
------------------------------------------------------
Implement Step 1 (bang-bang) first.
Experiment with different DEADBAND values:
  DEADBAND = 5    → motor oscillates a lot
  DEADBAND = 50   → motor stops further from target
  DEADBAND = 20   → a reasonable balance

What's the trade-off between oscillation and accuracy?

------------------------------------------------------
EXERCISE 2 — Tune kP  (★★★☆)
------------------------------------------------------
Switch to Step 2 (P-only control).
Experiment with these kP values and observe the behavior:
  kP = 0.0001  → very slow to reach target
  kP = 0.01    → overshoots and oscillates
  kP = 0.001   → often a good starting point

Document: what kP gives you fast response without oscillation?

------------------------------------------------------
EXERCISE 3 — Add I Term  (★★★☆)
------------------------------------------------------
Implement Step 3 (full PID).
Start with kI = 0.

Does the motor stop exactly at the target with P-only?
If there's still a small steady-state error (it stops slightly
off-target), increase kI gradually until it reaches the target.

Typical starting range: kI = 0.00001 to 0.001

------------------------------------------------------
EXERCISE 4 — Built-In vs. Your Own  (★★★★)
------------------------------------------------------
Compare your custom PID to the built-in RUN_TO_POSITION:

  motor.setMode(DcMotor.RunMode.RUN_TO_POSITION);
  motor.setTargetPosition(desiredPosition);
  motor.setPower(0.8);

Which is faster? Which is smoother? Which is easier to tune?
Run both on the same motor and compare.

(This directly mirrors the discussion in book section 24.5.4)

------------------------------------------------------
EXERCISE 5 — CHALLENGE: Feedforward  (★★★★)
------------------------------------------------------
If you have a VERTICAL mechanism (like a lift or arm that
fights gravity), add the kF term.

Method: Hold the mechanism still. With kP=kI=kD=0, find
the minimum motor power that holds it in place. That's your kF.

Add it as a constant and include it in the power calculation:
  power = (kP * error) + (kI * sumErrors) + (kD * derivative) + kF;
